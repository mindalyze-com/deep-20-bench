Deep20Bench · Twenty Questions for LLMs

Can an LLM ask its way to the answer?

Twenty Questions is the classic guessing game: one player chooses a hidden person, place, or thing; the other may ask only yes-or-no questions.

To win, a model needs world knowledge, a question strategy, and the ability to use every previous answer. Deep20Bench measures how efficiently it can do that.

Why this game works

Simple rules. Several abilities.

Playing well means deciding what information is missing, asking for it efficiently, and changing course after every answer.

01◎

World knowledge

Know which facts, categories, and distinctions are likely to matter.

02?

Question strategy

Choose questions that eliminate many possibilities instead of chasing random facts.

03≋

State tracking

Remember every YES, NO, and UNKNOWN, then use it in the next decision.

04✓

Decision discipline

Make an exact guess within the rules and before the question limit.

From game to benchmark

The same game, made comparable.

Every model plays behind the same information boundary and every completed run leaves a public record.

Why trust the comparison

Controlled and inspectable.

The score stays compact, while the evidence behind it remains available for closer inspection.

01▣

Strict isolation

The Guesser sees its category, prior actions, and final answer tokens—not the subject or supporting evidence.

02◉

Failures stay visible

Failed identifications receive a declared penalty. Invalid structured outputs cost turns and remain marked.

03◇

Every score is inspectable

Published results link to subjects, episodes, model settings, transcripts, cost, and public run data.

Origin

From a holiday game to a benchmark.

The idea for Deep20Bench was sparked during a holiday while Patrick Heusser and Markus Tuor were playing Twenty Questions with the kids. Inspired by the game, Patrick went on to design and build the benchmark from the ground up.
