Prototype · Twenty Questions for AI models

How well can AI models play Twenty Questions?

Deep20Bench tests how well AI models play the guessing game where one player thinks of a person or character and the other has to narrow it down with yes-or-no questions.

How scoring works

The model is told nothing but the broad category (for example, "video game character") and gets up to 50 questions - more than the traditional twenty, giving models more room to finish a round. Each question, wrong guess, or reply that does not follow the required format adds one point. If the model never finds the answer, the round scores 51. Scores are averaged across all rounds, and lower is better.

Current pilot

All results, game transcripts, and scoring data are public. The current pilot compares 15 model versions and settings across seven subjects, with five rounds per subject. The concept works and the first step is complete. Expanding the pilot is straightforward; cost is the main constraint.

What this pilot tests

A focused test of adaptive questioning.

Deep20Bench tests whether a model can turn broad knowledge into a useful sequence of questions, retain the answers, and commit to an exact guess.

What it measures

Question quality, use of prior answers, and success before the turn limit.

Why it matters

What it does not claim

How the game is played

The Guesser asks. Three roles determine the answer.

1

Ask

The Guesser is the LLM under test. It asks up to 50 counted yes-or-no questions and makes the final guess.

2

Check

An Oracle searches the live web and cites evidence. A blind Reviewer makes a second decision on every YES or NO. A blind Judge resolves disagreement.

3

Score

Lower is better. A failed trial contributes 51 questions. Subjects carry equal weight.

Core control

The model under test sees only the game.

It receives the category, its prior actions, and final YES, NO, or UNKNOWN tokens. It never sees private adjudicator prompts, evidence, searches, or hidden subject data.
