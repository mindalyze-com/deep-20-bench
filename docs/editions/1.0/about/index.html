A shared idea, built into a benchmark.

Patrick Heusser and Markus Tuor came up with Deep20Bench while playing Twenty Questions with the kids. Patrick then designed and built the benchmark.

What’s new.

New models, releases, and articles. A score is the average number of questions per round, with a fixed penalty for failed rounds. Lower is better.

29 October 2026

DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 (high) joins the leaderboard.

It finished edition 1.1 with a question score of 22.70, 21st of 23 models, solving 25 of 30 rounds. Five rounds failed and five format errors were recorded. gpt-oss 120B (high) scored 22.23. DeepSeek scored 9.33 on Albert Einstein, slightly below leader GPT-6 Astra’s 9.67, but 40.00 on Bike pump against 26.33. Eyebrow and Door handle accounted for four of its five failed rounds. The tested model cost $0.30 over 30 rounds, excluding answer checks.

28 October 2026

GPT-6.1 Terra (high) joins the leaderboard.

It averaged 18.83 questions per round in edition 1.1, 14th of 22 models, with 3 failed rounds out of 30 and no format errors. GPT-6.1 Sol (high) averaged 15.23. Terra needed fewer questions on Door handle (28.00 against 33.33), but more on Albert Schweitzer (39.33 against 19.33), where two rounds failed. The tested model cost $0.57 over 30 rounds, compared with Sol’s $1.13. These costs exclude answer checks and the earlier attempts of rounds repeated after technical failures.

23 October 2026

Claude Haiku 5.5 (high) joins the leaderboard.

It averaged 20.00 questions per round in edition 1.1, 15th of 21 models, with 5 failed rounds out of 30. Compared with GPT-6 Luna (high) at 21.17, it needed fewer questions on seven of the ten subjects, with clear leads on Garfield and Achilles. Spider web was its weak spot: 30, 38, and 21 questions, against Luna’s 16, 22, and 15.

14 October 2026

Gemini 4 Argon (high) matches the lowest average score.

It averaged 13.67 questions per round in edition 1.1, the same as GPT-6 Astra (high): both used 410 questions over 30 rounds and solved every round. Astra ranks first because its 95% confidence interval is narrower. Argon was the best of 20 models on Door handle and second on Albert Schweitzer and Bike pump, but 19th on Moon, which took it 11, 12, and 14 questions.
